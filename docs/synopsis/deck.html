Personal Agentic RAG

Fast and slow thinking for your studies and documents: private, measurable and free.

“Handle your documents safely and securely.”

Mohit & Kunal · Professor: Dr. Neetu Verma · DCRUST, Murthal

A private, open-source agentic RAG for a student's notes, books and documents. The core idea is to combine fast System-1 decisions with slow System-2 reasoning, and to measure it properly.

The problem

Students’ knowledge is scattered, and AI doesn’t help safely

Scattered material

Notes, books, slides and past papers are spread across folders and phones

Hard to find

Marksheets, certificates and exam dates when you need them

Ungrounded answers

Chatbots answer from the internet, not your syllabus, and don’t cite the page

Privacy cost

Cloud AI only works if your documents leave your control

Goal: an assistant that is grounded, private and measurable.

Personal Agentic RAG · 2

Four problems: scattered material, documents that are hard to find, answers not grounded in your own material, and privacy. The project answers all four, and measures how well it does.

The core idea

Think fast when you can, slow when you must

System 1 · Fast

  • Small model with typed answers + confidence
  • Routes questions, answers simple facts
  • Safety checks on every input and output
  • No LLM call

System 2 · Slow

  • LLM agent that plans and reasons
  • Searches notes, books and documents
  • Several steps, cites its sources
  • Used only when System 1 is unsure

Inspired by Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011)

Personal Agentic RAG · 3

Humans answer easy questions instantly and think hard only when needed. We copy that: a small, fast, calibrated System-1 model handles easy and safety decisions, and escalates to the slow LLM agent only when its confidence is low.

Personal Agentic RAG · 4

System 1 always runs first: an input guard, then a router that gives an intent and a confidence. If it's confident and the fact exists, it answers directly. Otherwise it escalates to the System-2 agent. System-2 answers pass back through System-1 checks for grounding and privacy before the user sees them.

Two examples

Same assistant, two ways of thinking

FAST PATH · SYSTEM 1

“When is my DBMS exam?”

  1. Guard: safe
  2. Router: fact lookup, high confidence
  3. Answer from the facts table
  4. Cites the exam timetable PDF

No LLM call

SLOW PATH · SYSTEM 2

“Compare how my notes and Galvin explain paging”

  1. Router: comparison, so escalate
  2. Agent searches notes, then the book
  3. Grounding check passes
  4. Cites notes p. 14 and Galvin ch. 9

Personal Agentic RAG · 5

The fast path is cheap and instant but only works for structured facts. The slow path handles real reasoning across several sources. Measuring when and how often each path is used is the core of our evaluation.

Personal Agentic RAG · 6

Two kinds of data. The study library holds academic material with page-level citations. The personal vault holds sensitive documents, encrypted at rest; facts like exam dates and CGPA are extracted into a table that powers the System-1 fast path.

Guardrails and security

Three System-1 guards, plus a locked vault

G1 · INPUT

Injection guard

Blocks attacks in questions and in text hidden inside uploaded PDFs

G2 · OUTPUT

Grounding check

Is every claim supported by a cited source? If not, retry or say “I don’t know”

G3 · OUTPUT

PII leak check

Never reveals ID numbers or marks you didn’t ask for

AES-256 encrypted vault

Local models by default

Audit log + red-team tests

Personal Agentic RAG · 7

Guardrails are System-1 decisions too: fast typed checks with a confidence score. We test them with about 100 red-team attacks and report how many get through, and how often the guards wrongly refuse a normal question.

Personal Agentic RAG · 8

Each request becomes a tree of spans: System-1 guards and router decisions, System-2 agent steps and tool calls, then the final checks. This makes the system debuggable and gives the raw data for the evaluation.

Personal Agentic RAG · 9

These five questions turn the project from a demo into a study with measurable results. jeff is an open-source, self-hosted implementation of the Jev System-1 API; our alternative is a small SetFit classifier trained on synthetic data.

Evaluation

Public data, clear baselines

Datasets

  • “Alex Demo”: a fictional student’s documents
  • Open textbooks (OpenStax)
  • ~200 Q&A with gold pages
  • ~100 red-team attacks

Compared systems

  • Plain RAG
  • System 2 only (agent)
  • System 1 + System 2 (ours)
  • Ours without guardrails
  • jeff vs SetFit engine

Metrics

  • Correctness, faithfulness (Ragas)
  • Page-citation accuracy
  • Latency, LLM calls
  • Calibration (ECE)
  • Attack success, false refusals

One command (make eval) reproduces every table and plot. No real personal data is used.
